# Executive summary

## Objective
Determine how casual riders and annual members use Cyclistic differently and translate those differences into actionable membership-conversion tests.

## What the data says
- **3,818,004 rides** were analyzed across a complete January-December 2019 cycle.
- Members account for **76.9%** of trips; casual riders account for **23.1%**.
- Casual median ride duration is **25.8 minutes**, versus **9.8 minutes** for members.
- **43.0%** of casual rides occur on weekends, more than double the member weekend share (**18.5%**).
- Casual riders concentrate **56.0%** of annual rides in summer.
- Same-station trips represent **11.9%** of casual rides vs **1.6%** of member rides.
- Member weekday activity is more concentrated in 7-9am / 4-6pm windows (**58.5%** of weekday member trips vs **37.4%** for casual).

## Recommended actions
1. **Geo-target high-casual stations.** Prioritize Lake Shore Dr & Monroe St, Streeter Dr & Grand Ave, Shedd Aquarium, Field Museum, Dusable Harbor and other high-volume, high-casual locations for conversion experiments.
2. **Align media timing with casual demand.** Put more acquisition effort into weekends, warm months, and midday/afternoon casual windows; run a separate weekday treatment for commute-time casual demand.
3. **Test segment-specific membership messages.** Leisure/round-trip contexts should receive different creative from recurring weekday contexts. Measure conversion through privacy-safe first-party campaign data and controlled experiments.

## Important caveat
The trip dataset measures behavior, not motivation or conversion. These recommendations identify high-potential contexts for experiments; they do not establish causal drivers of membership.
